# Assignment Brief — Case Study AI Tool & LinkedIn Post
FIN 3010 Principles of Finance | Prof. Marcus Painter

**Due:** URL of each post submitted to Canvas by **11:59 PM, Wednesday Dec 2**.
**Format:** Partners build the tool together; each of us writes and posts our **own** version on our own LinkedIn and credits the other by name.
**Audience:** a recruiter or working professional who has never heard of this class.
**Length:** ~300–600 words. Shorter and sharper is better.

## The post must
1. **Open with the question** — the case problem and why it matters to anyone in finance.
2. **Say what we built** — which AI tool, what we asked it to do, what we changed when the first attempt didn't work.
3. **Show one real result** — at least one visual (chart, table, screenshot) *and a link to the webpage*.
4. **Say where AI got it wrong** — one specific error/hallucination/oversimplification and how we verified it.
5. **Close with what's next** — what we'd build with more time, or how this changed how we use AI.
6. **Tags:** #FIN3010 #SLU #Finance, plus tag Prof. Painter (linkedin.com/in/marcuspainter) and the Chaifetz Department of Finance (linkedin.com/company/slufinance).

## Grading — 40 points
- [ ] Explains the case problem and the finance concept behind it
- [ ] Describes the tool and how we prompted our way there
- [ ] Shows a concrete result (chart/table/screenshot **and link to webpage**)
- [ ] Names one thing AI got wrong or oversimplified, and how we addressed it
- [ ] Reads as a professional public post: clear, tight, no typos

## Prof's emphasis
- Verify every number against the original source — it carries our names.
- Be specific about AI's role ("used Claude to pull 2026 capex guidance from four earnings releases"), not "leveraged AI to unlock insights."
- Don't overclaim. Say it's a back-of-the-envelope model built in a week.
